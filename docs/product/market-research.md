# Market Research

**Research date:** 2026-10-13.
**Method:** Official product and pricing pages were fetched directly (read-only) and converted to text for analysis. No independent review sites or app-store listings were retrieved, so feature lists should be treated as vendor claims unless noted. Pricing shown is what each vendor publishes on the page cited; promotional or app-store prices may differ.

## Feature patterns across competitors
- **Core:** multi-account aggregation, automatic categorization, budgets, transaction search, reports/trends, recurring detection, net worth tracking.
- **Differentiators:** YNAB method and shared "Together" groups; Monarch's ad-free all-in-one net worth and forecasting; Emma's UK cashback/savings/crypto and Spaces; PocketGuard's bill negotiation and subscription cancellation; Rocket Money's human-assisted cancellation/negotiation; Spendee's shared wallets; Snoop's UK-specific money-saving actions.
- **Monetization:** most charge a monthly/annual subscription; several gate bank connectivity or advanced insights behind paid tiers; free tiers are typically limited in account count, history depth, or features.
- **UK market:** Snoop and Emma emphasize open-banking-powered insights, bill switching, cashback, and credit scores.

## Market patterns (observed)
- Paid personal finance apps cluster around £4–£15/month, with annual discounts common.
- Bank aggregation is a core feature in this category but is operationally expensive and often bundled with paid tiers.
- Vendors increasingly position privacy ("we never sell your data") as a differentiator against ad-funded free apps.
- AI/assistants appear as premium add-ons (e.g., Expensify Concierge, Moneyhub data insights), but basic categorization and rule engines remain deterministic.

## Unmet needs and opportunities (labelled as analysis, not verified user research)
1. **Explainable cash-flow foresight for irregular income** — most tools optimize a fixed monthly budget; fewer answer "when will my account go negative before the next payday?"
2. **Household collaboration that separates shared and personal space** — several products share budgets but are less strong at personal-vs-shared boundaries within one account.
3. **Low-friction manual capture for people who do not connect banks** — bank sync dominates, while manual capture UX is often secondary.
4. **Transparent pricing and data control** — users increasingly distrust "free" finance apps; explicit data-export/deletion and clear limits are a differentiator.
5. **Multi-currency households and cross-border spending** — repeatedly under-served in consumer tools; YNAB explicitly does not support multiple currencies in one plan.

## Verification notes
- Pricing figures above were read from the cited official pages on 2026-10-13 and may change or vary by region/app store.
- Emma, Wallet by BudgetBakers, Goodbudget, Rocket Money, Moneyhub, and Expensify did not expose clear pricing on the fetched pages; their prices are **unverified** in this research.
- Independent user-review and complaint data was not collected in this phase and should be gathered before finalizing pricing or differentiation claims.
