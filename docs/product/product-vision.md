# Product Vision

**Status:** Proposal — guides future development but does not authorize implementation.

## Purpose
ExpenseTracker will evolve from a manual personal expense tracker into a trustworthy personal finance platform delivered across Android, iOS, and web. The product helps people capture transactions with low friction, understand spending, plan budgets, and make better money decisions — without requiring them to adopt an elaborate budgeting methodology.

## Target audience
- Individuals who currently manage money manually in spreadsheets or notes and want a low-friction, privacy-respecting tool.
- UK and European consumers who value transparent pricing and are wary of ad-funded "free" finance apps.
- Couples and households who need a shared view of budgets, expenses, and goals.
- Early-career professionals and side-income earners with irregular income who need cash-flow foresight rather than rigid zero-based budgeting.

## Customer problems
- Transactions are tedious to record, so people abandon tracking.
- Monthly totals exist but do not answer "will I run out of money before payday?"
- Budgets are created once and ignored; they do not adapt to irregular income.
- Households have no lightweight way to share finances without sharing a bank account.
- Users do not trust finance apps that monetize their transaction data.

## Value proposition
A privacy-first personal finance companion that makes transaction capture effortless, keeps budgeting simple, and gives plain-language, explainable answers about future cash flow — across mobile and web, with a genuinely useful free tier.

## Positioning statement
For individuals and households who want control over their money without complexity, ExpenseTracker is a personal finance platform that combines fast manual tracking, simple budgets, and clear cash-flow foresight — unlike ad-funded apps, it protects financial data, and unlike rigid budgeting tools, it adapts to irregular income.

## Long-term direction
- Phase 1–2: reliable manual tracking, budgeting, and a web experience with SaaS foundations.
- Phase 3–4: recurring transactions, alerts, richer reporting, savings goals, forecasting, and household collaboration.
- Phase 5–6: optional regulated bank connectivity, intelligent categorization, explainable AI insights, and specialist segments (freelancers, multi-currency households).

## Non-goals
- ExpenseTracker is not a bank, lender, or investment adviser; it provides budgeting insights, not regulated financial advice.
- It does not aim to become an enterprise spend-management product (e.g., Expensify) in the near term.
- AI is a means to an end, not a headline feature; deterministic, explainable features are preferred where they are sufficient.

## Guardrails
- Free tier must remain genuinely useful.
- Security, privacy, data export, and deletion are foundational, never premium-only.
- Users always retain access to their own financial data.
- Paid features must deliver meaningful additional value and be transparently priced.
